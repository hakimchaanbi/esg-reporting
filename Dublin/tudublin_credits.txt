TU Dublin Sustainability Intelligence team (Ref1) leads the Digital Transformation programme of the university on sustainability themes. The team looks at gathering all data from all the university to assess and report on the sustainability level of the university, and develop new digital methodology to enhance and consolidate the assessment and report on all sustainability aspects. The sustainability digitalisation programme already produced three web applications that enable to assess the sustainability in the curriculum (analysing the curriculum databases), sustainability in research (analysing all research outputs in the TU Dublin research repository and Scopus), and sustainability in communication (analysing website content). --- narrative ---
An extensive mapping exercise identifying which people collect, process and validate information behind each STARS questions has been conducted. The supply chain of information was tracked as information was seeked. A second mapping exercise looked at identifying which IT system holds which pieces of information requested in STARS or exploitable to report in STARS. Each stakeholder were given the user manual, the section of the user manual relevant to them, STARS Tracking Worksheet templates complemented with all the credit criteria information and examples provided in the manual. STARS Tracking Worksheets were customised to groups of stakeholders with people designated to input and function/department leads to review. Meetings were organised with each stakeholder (including student union representatives) to introduce the questions and approach, and follow up to address any concern and issues. When dealing with external suppiers/contractors required to report a large amount of data (e.g. list of food and beverages, and other items purchased, as well as the sustainability certificates of each supplier) new templates were design to take the data available and treat it to report on STARS criteria. This engagement formed a training to suppliers on how to address sustainability questions. All progress updates and issues were reported to the University Sustainability Council gathering leaders and student representatives who enabled the data collection process. Overall about 60 people contributed to the data collection and validation.

--- narrative ---
A sustainability language model to classify text among the 17 UN SDG and identify the sustainability level as Sustainability-Focused, Sustainability-Inclusive, and Non-Sustainable has been developped and patented. The model accuracy was tested and validated for short and long text, and compared to state-of-the-art models. The sustainability language model developped in >88% accurate across all SDGs for both long and short text, and represent a significant advancement of the sate-of-the-art which had an accuracy of only 88% for short text, and 76% for long text. While the results provided by the model have been manually validated by sustainability champions, the results are in the process of being further manually reviewed by document owners (e.g. module coordinator/owner, school and faculty leads, academic affairs; authors of research papers; communication officers managing a TU Dublin website section) with some guidance on what sustainability and SDG definitions and background knowledge. The model will evolve with user feedback while also educating users on sustainability aspect, providing a systematic and consolidated means to assess, review, and report sustainability.
All TU Dublin module/course descriptors including the module code, title, overview, learning outcomes, assessments, department owners/coordinators, and NFQ level were retrieved from our Programme and Module Catalogue (PMC). The sustainability level per SDG of each module was analysed using our validated sustainability language model. The analysis could then report on the sustainbility of modules/courses offered by each TU Dublin department.
