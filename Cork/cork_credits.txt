--- narrative ---
In 2010, UCC was the first university in the world to be awarded a Green Flag from the Foundation for Environmental Education. In 2023, we were the first University globally to renew our Green Flag for the 5th time. The combination of bottom-up activism from our student community and top down commitment from University leadership has been key to our success. The programme was started by students that wanted to have a say in environmental management on campus. They made contact with the national operator of the Green Flag programme in Ireland and university management and agreed a pilot Green Campus initiative. The UCC Green Campus committee is co-chaired by a member of the UCC Environmental Society and the Students Union Environmental and Sustainability Rep; it has membership from staff and students and meets regularly throughout the year to design new projects and campaigns. Sustainability is now at the core of UCC’s strategies and values, and the Green Campus programme is seen as an exemplar in driving cultural change.
At UCC, Education for Sustainable Development presents multiple entry points for staff: some connect naturally with the SDGs, while others work through the lens of competencies such as systems thinking, critical reflection, or normative values. In 2024 the University appointed its first ESD Officer, based within the Office for the Integration of Research, Teaching and Learning. The Officer supports teaching staff to identify opportunities for ESD integration. Their work is complemented by dedicated resources developed at UCC including the SDG teaching toolkit: https://www.ucc.ie/en/sdg-toolkit/
Pedagogically, UCC emphasises transformative learning through the “head, heart, and hands” model—building knowledge, engaging emotions and values, and enabling practical action. This is realised through methods such as case-based learning, project-based learning, cooperative inquiry, and community-based research. Examples including postgraduate modules in community-based participatory research, where students co-design projects with local organisations, and creative approaches in the humanities, such as applying sustainability frameworks to the study of German literature.
Mapping exercises reveal that 75% of UCC’s undergraduate programmes now explicitly reference the SDGs, particularly Goals 3, 4, and 5. This stepwise integration—ranging from introductory engagement to full programme design—shows how ESD can be embedded progressively across disciplines.
In 2024, UCC also required all Programme Directors to include the SDGs that their courses cover when completing their course details on Courseleaf.
A presentation on thesea aspects can be found here: https://pressbooks.oer.ie/hea-esd-spotlight-series-case-studies-compendium/chapter/esd-in-action-practices-and-approaches-at-ucc/
In Febraury 2022, UCC together with Queen's University Belfast launched SDSN Ireland, an all island network aimed at leveraging HEIs and NGOs across the island of Ireland to accelerate implementation of the 2030 Agenda and its SDGs. SDSN Ireland members collaborate to co-create and implement integrated solutions that drive SDG progress, with a strong focus on accelerating policy development and tangible impact. This includes fostering cooperation between the North and South of Ireland to maximize outcomes. Three thematic groups have been formed, on the subjects of "Research and Policy", "Arts and Sustainability" and "Zero Carbon Campus".
On Tuesday 2 December, SDSN Ireland and University College Cork supported the 2nd National SDG Stakeholder Forum of 2025 under the banner “SDG Localisation – SDGs Working Locally”. This hybrid event, hosted the Government of Ireland’s Department of Climate, Energy and Environment, brought together policymakers, academics, community stakeholders and civil society from across Ireland with a shared focus: how to turn global ambitions into real local impact.
The day of presentations, panel discussions and interactive workshops underscored the urgent need to root the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development in local communities — putting the “global” into locally meaningful action.

--- narrative ---
From 2023 to 2026 UCC partnered with several other Universities across Europe on the SDG4U project. More information on the project is available here: SDG4U – SDG4U
As part of this project each partner conducted an awareness survey to assess knowledge, beliefs, and practices of university students, faculty, and staff. The survey questions were designed by the consortium partners who comprised researchers, professors, and heads of sustainability. The survey was administered in 2024, with plans to repeat the survey in 2026 in order to understand changes and trends over time. The survey consists of multiple-choice questions that are aimed at assessing the level of awareness on general sustainability issues, attitudes and perceptions as well as to provide a prioritization in the implementation of the SDGs in each university.
The assessment showed that students had a strong understanding of the basic definition of sustainability and tended to find the environmental dimension more important. Students exhibited a strong foundational knowledge of sustainability principles and expressed a high interest in sustainable development, significant gaps exist in translating this awareness into deeper comprehension of systemic issues, institutional action, and personal behavioral change.
The majority of respondents correctly identify sustainability's core premise (living within environmental limits) and recognize the interconnected pillars of environmental, social, and economic development. However, understanding appears fragmented when examining specific mechanisms.
A more detailed analysis and overview is available here: https://www.sdg4uproject.eu/resources/
The Questionnaire was designed by the project partner, American College of Greece, and shared with all partners. Feedback was provided by the project team at UCC, which included the Head of Sustainability and ESD Officer. The first pilot application of the questionnaire took place at the American College of Greece in December 2024. The responses to this questionnaire were also used as means to improve the questions.
As part of this project a post awareness survey was also designed by the project partners. The survey consists of multiple-choice questions, some of which are generic on the SDGs and questions aligned with specific SDGs (1 question per SDG). For the latter group of questions, a single correct answer is defined. The questions are designed so that there is balanced coverage of environmental, social, and governance dimensions, while common misconceptions have been included in the identified options as distractors in order to assess the depth of knowledge and the existing perceptions on the 17 SDGs. The survey is designed in such a way as to allow for statistical analysis.

The Sustainability Institute uses a global challenge-based approach to respond to, and find research solutions for, the three core environmental challenges of Climate Action, Circular Economy, and Healthy Environment. The Environmental Research Institute (ERI) was established in 2000 by University College Cork as a flagship institute to integrate researchers from over 20 different disciplines addressing the multi-faceted nature of sustainability.
In 2025, the Environmental Research Institute became the Sustainability Institute, and with this change comes a renewed ambition to tackle the complex, interconnected challenges of climate change, biodiversity loss, and resource scarcity through transformative research, innovation, and education that drive real-world impact.
The Sustainability Institute harnesses the University’s strengths in interdisciplinarity, research excellence, impactful scholarship, and leadership to tackle the complexity of today’s global sustainability challenges. These challenges demand more than “research-as-usual” - they require deep collaboration across disciplines and sectors to create usable knowledge and robust solutions.
We are committed to actionable, solutions-focused research; the formation of interdisciplinary teams spanning the natural and social sciences, business, law, and humanities; and the development of long-term partnerships with stakeholders in government, industry, NGOs, and communities.
The Institute fosters a dynamic research environment where diverse expertise is integrated to address environmental challenges in a truly collaborative and synergistic way. From fundamental discovery to applied solutions, our work is driven by the goal of delivering real-world impact—advancing environmental, social, and economic sustainability.
The Insitute is home to 480 research staff representing 23 schools, with 120 PhD students.
The Institute for Social Science in the 21st Century (ISS21) at UCC promoteS interdisciplinary research through research clusters, affiliated projects, events and cross-university collaborations. These research clusters include "Research for Civil Society, Environment and Social Action (REACT)", which brings together researchers with interests in civil society, from disciplines such as Applied Social Studies, Cooperative Studies, Geography and Law. Cluster researchers are members of the Irish Civil Society Research Network.
An overview of cluster events, research projects and membership can be found here: https://www.ucc.ie/en/iss21/clusters/civil-society/

The survey consists of multiple-choice questions that are aimed at assessing the level of awareness on general sustainability issues, attitudes and perceptions as well as to provide a prioritization in the implementation of the SDGs in each university.
An interesting finding was that while participants cited waste reduction activites as the ones that thye engage with most frequently, SDG 12 (Sustainable Consumption and Production) still came out as the SDG that they thought required the most action by the insitution. This reflect a trend that has long been noted, that waste as an aspect that is particularly visable tends to get a lot of attention.
SDG 13 was the second most cited as requiring further action from the University. An interesting finding was that, alongside environmental aspects of sustainability, many particpants viewed fair working conditions for staff as being equally important, demonstrating a need for the University to perhaps become more alinged in terms of its actions on environmental and social sustainability.
The main barriers to action highlighted were resistance to change and a lack of awareness and concern.
As part of this project a post awareness survey was also designed by the project partners. The survey consists of multiple-choice questions, a selection of which relate specifically to University actions on sustainability, including what particpiants engage with most actively and what actions they think the University should prioritise.

The Crow’s Nest site is a well-known landmark in Cork, the site of a famous public house of the same name located at the junction of city and hinterland where Wilton Road meets the Lee valley at Carrigrohane Road. The new Crow’s Nest development takes the form of 5 slender vertical elements rising from a plinth or podium containing a students’ garden, set over an entrance and reception zone at street level, open and welcoming to the public as well as to the student population. The building varies in height from ten storeys to seven storeys, with a smaller gateway element sheltering a lift and generous staircase linking entrance to podium level and mediating the scale between two storey houses on Wilton Road and the new building. The accommodation includes 255 student bed-spaces, ancillary student facilities, a new university health centre and a café opening onto the Carrigrohane Road. Extensive student common facilities and amenities are incorporated including student common areas, laundry, study rooms, music rooms and private external spaces. The project has been designed to exemplary sustainability standards in line with UCC’s ISO 50001 accreditation, (the first third level institution in the world to reach this standard). The building achieves an A3 rating under the Building Energy Rating scheme and has been designed to the BREEAM “Excellent” rating and compliant with the Nearly Zero Energy Building (NZEB) standard. To achieve these standards the building’s fabric has been designed to achieve exceptionally high levels of thermal performance, far in excess of current building regulations. Extensive photo-voltaic solar panels on the roof of each block lower the building's requirement to draw energy from the grid and high efficiency heat recovery ventilation is utilized where natural ventilation is not possible. High efficiency LED lighting is used throughout, together with intelligent lighting controls to reduce power consumption. The building incorporates a rainwater harvesting system to provide grey water for the development and is fitted with water saving devices throughout. The project was completed in July 2023. See BREEAM certification document attached below.
The new Squad Gym at UCC’s Mardyke Arena (420m2) was completed in March 2023 and has demonstrated that utilizing digital technologies, sustainable materials and modern methods of construction can deliver hugely positive results to the construction sector, the built environment and beyond. https://buildingirelandmagazine.com/summerhill-construction-raises-the-bar-with-mardyke-arena-extension/

The Higher Education Access Route to Education (HEAR) is a third-level alternative admissions scheme which offers places on reduced points, and extra college supports to school leavers from socio-economically disadvantaged backgrounds who are resident in the Republic of Ireland. HEAR Applicants must meet a range of financial, social and cultural indicators to be considered for a reduced points place and extra college support. Applicants must meet the minimum entry requirements and achieve a minimum of 300 points; Applicants must achieve at least 85% of the points required for the course and meet any additional requirements, where they exist.
The UCC PLUS Programme (Providing Links to Underrepresented School Leavers) looks after HEAR-eligible students who are offered a place at University College Cork. UCC PLUS+ empowers HEAR students to participate fully in student life and reach their full potential.

University of Sanctuary activities are advanced and coordinated with the support of a Sanctuary Subcommittee which reports to the ULT Global Engagement Committee.

In May 2024, University College Cork set up an Emergency Response Working Group, as one of a number of commitments to support the education of Palestinian students and scholars displaced or at risk as a result of the conflict in Gaza. The aim of the Group was to report to the President on a programme of work to address the immediate educational needs of Palestinian students and scholars displaced or at risk and examine how UCC can provide support. The work of the Group took place against the backdrop of critical and urgent need in Gaza and taking account of the University of Sanctuary framework.

In April 2025, UCC launched its first "Digital Cleanup Day", which included an awareness campaign with information sessions and practical workshops, enabling individuals to learn about how to reach "Inbox Zero" and a guide to undertaking a Digital Declutter. Every School and function was encouraged to pledge a day between April and June to dedicate to their collective digital decluttering effort. A total of 820 hours were pledged. UCC Digital Clean-Up Day is part of a global movement. The initiative was run through the Continuous Improvement Action Network in collaboration with The Students’ Union, Sustainability and Climate Action, IT Services, Centre for Digital Education, Marketing and Brand Impact and Staff Development and Wellbeing.
More information is available here https://www.ucc.ie/en/sustainability-climate-action/news/uccs-first-digital-cleanup-day.html
In 2026 the Digital Declutter has been upgraded to a week of activities aimed at highlighting the impact of our digital footprint and ways to remedy this.

The UCC Sustainability Academy is the first university sustainability training and education unit of its kind in Ireland which uses a unique model for sustainability education delivery. The Academy programmes draw on the deep expertise of over 100 sustainability academics in UCC across 25 Schools combined with a core team of sustainability trainers and industry practitioners to contribute to programmes. We pride ourselves on delivering educational content which is cutting-edge based on the research output of 400 research papers annually within the internationally leading UCC Environmental research Institute.
The Sustainability Academy programmes are UCC accredited and can be used for attaining professional CPD recognition.
One of the most innovative offerings was a Climate Diplomacy Training course delivered to the Irish Department of Foreign Affairs staff. The course was aimed at supporting diplomatic staff in offices across the globe to better understand climate change and their role in supporting climate action.
